## From an idea to a publication

When I joined the Civil Engineering Scientific Association at the University of Tehran, I noticed a gap in the association's long history of student activities: it had not published a magazine for more than twelve years. Together with my friend Mehdi, I saw an opportunity to create more than a conventional student magazine.

We wanted Chamroosh to become a platform where civil engineering could be discussed through different perspectives — connecting technical knowledge with history, culture, education, professional experience, and critical thinking.

From the beginning, Mehdi and I initiated the project and worked together to bring the publication to life. I took the lead in developing its concept, goals, name, editorial structure, and overall direction, while working with the editorial and design teams to turn the idea into a functioning publication.

Chamroosh received its official publication license in Fall 2024.

## Finding the name

The name **Chamroosh** was inspired by a mythical bird in Iranian mythology associated with Mount Damavand.

I was drawn to the idea of Chamroosh as a figure that could observe, connect, and carry stories across time. It became a metaphor for the publication itself: looking at civil engineering from different perspectives while connecting Iran's engineering past, present, and future.

The name was also inspired by the works and classes of Dr. Jaleh Amoozgar, creating a connection between Iranian mythology, literature, and the academic environment.

## Building the visual identity

The visual identity was also part of the original concept I developed for Chamroosh.

I wanted the identity to connect Iranian heritage with a contemporary academic publication. The logo concept was built around **Chamroosh as a bird in flight**, combined with the Persian letter **"چ"** from the publication's name.

Iranian turquoise was selected as the primary color, referencing Iranian visual culture while creating a sense of calm, clarity, and openness.

The detailed **page design, typography, and editorial layouts were developed by Velgard Studio**, the graphic design team working with the publication. Together, the conceptual identity and its visual execution created a consistent language across the magazine.

## An editorial structure with four perspectives

Rather than organizing Chamroosh around a single type of content, I developed four recurring sections, each representing a different way of looking at civil engineering.

**Yeki Bood — Once There Was**  
Stories from the history of civil engineering, important people, projects, and experiences that connect the present with the past.

**Yeki Nabood — Once There Wasn't**  
Emerging technologies, unconventional ideas, and new approaches that challenge established ways of thinking.

**Noghte Nazar — Point of View**  
Opinions and experiences from students, professors, engineers, and industry professionals, creating a space for dialogue between different generations and perspectives.

**Jaye Khali — The Missing Piece**  
Critical discussions about gaps and challenges in civil engineering and the built environment, moving from identifying problems toward imagining possible solutions.

Together, these sections created a publication that could move between history and the future, technical knowledge and personal experience, and observation and criticism.

## Four editions, four conversations

The first issue established Chamroosh's editorial direction by bringing together historical stories, emerging technologies, student perspectives, and field research.

One of its historical stories explored **Taq Kasra**, using the project as a lens through which to discuss challenges that remain familiar in contemporary construction: procurement, technical evaluation, contractor selection, conflicts between project stakeholders, and project crises.

## Special Issue 01 — From reporting to criticism

The first special issue was created around the **Industry & Civil Engineering Conference at the University of Tehran**.

Rather than simply documenting the event, we covered the conference, its papers, and its posters while also paying attention to the reactions of students participating in the event.

When we noticed recurring criticism from students, we developed a questionnaire and conducted interviews to understand these concerns more systematically.

The result became a dedicated critical section within the special issue, turning event coverage into a platform for reflection and discussion.

## Special Issue 02 — Questioning education

The second special issue shifted the focus from engineering practice to the education of future engineers.

The project began with a simple question:

**What is missing from the way we teach civil engineering?**

The findings were compiled into a dedicated special issue and shared with students, faculty members, and the educational administration.

## Bringing students into the conversation

A major part of Chamroosh was giving students an active voice.

In **Noghte Nazar**, students responded to questions about structural strengthening, sustainable development, and the role of artificial intelligence in civil engineering risk management.

In **Jaye Khali**, we moved beyond online responses and conducted interviews directly at the faculty, asking students what they believed was missing from the cities and built environments they encountered every day.

The first issue collected responses from **40 students**, alongside **19 field interviews**.

## From magazine to community

Chamroosh gradually became more than a magazine.

After the first issue, students began reaching out to contribute scientific and cultural pieces. Professors, graduate students, and industry professionals also suggested projects, events, and achievements for future coverage.

The first issue was printed in a limited run of 100 copies and distributed among academics and professionals in the civil engineering community.

The digital editions were distributed freely through Telegram, allowing the publication to reach beyond the physical campus.

## My role

As the **founder and Editor-in-Chief of Chamroosh**, I was responsible for shaping the project from its earliest idea to its publication and public presence.

My role included:

- Developing the original concept and goals of the publication
- Choosing the name **Chamroosh** and developing its conceptual story
- Defining the editorial structure and four main sections
- Developing the core visual identity and logo concept
- Shaping the content and direction of each edition
- Coordinating the editorial team and contributors
- Developing stories, interviews, and research topics
- Conducting and coordinating interviews
- Working with Velgard Studio on page design, typography, and editorial layouts
- Creating and managing social media content with my friend **Farnam**
- Overseeing the publication process from concept to distribution

The project brought together several aspects of the way I like to work: **research, storytelling, design, communication, project coordination, and engineering.**

Chamroosh became an experiment in what a student-led publication could become when it was treated not simply as a magazine, but as a platform for creating conversations.
