After learning the fundamentals of machine learning, I wanted to better understand one of the most important steps before a model is trained: **how we represent data**.

I took Kaggle's **Feature Engineering** course to explore how raw data can be transformed into features that make machine learning models more useful and informative.

The course introduced techniques such as feature creation, mutual information, clustering, principal component analysis, and target encoding.

What I found particularly interesting was the role of **domain knowledge**. Machine learning does not remove the need to understand the problem we are trying to solve. The way we represent a problem through data can have a major influence on what a model is able to learn.

This is especially relevant to engineering, where datasets can contain many different types of information about buildings, infrastructure, construction projects, and cities.

### What I learned

- Feature creation
- Mutual information
- Categorical variables
- K-Means clustering
- Principal Component Analysis (PCA)
- Target encoding

### Why I took it

I wanted to understand what happens between collecting data and training a machine learning model, and how engineering knowledge can help transform raw data into meaningful information.
