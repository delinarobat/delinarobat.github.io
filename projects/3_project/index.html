TEDxUT — Curating Shahram Mokri

Developing a filmmaker's ideas into a TED-style talk

Curating Shahram Mokri

TEDxUT · October 2024

As a member of the TEDxUT Curating Team, I had the opportunity to work directly with filmmaker Shahram Mokri to develop his ideas into a TED-style talk.

The curating process was not simply about preparing a speech. It was about listening carefully, identifying the ideas behind a speaker’s experiences, and helping shape them into a story that could communicate with an audience in a clear and engaging way.

From Ideas to a Story

My main responsibility was to help Shahram Mokri transform his ideas into a structure that worked within the format of a TED Talk.

During our meetings, we discussed his experiences, ideas, and perspectives on cinema. I tried to identify the strongest ideas and help connect them into a coherent narrative, while keeping the story authentic to his own voice.

The process required us to think about more than just what he wanted to say. We also considered how the audience would receive the idea, where the story should begin, how different points could connect, and what the audience should take away from the talk.

### The Curating Process The process included several stages: - Listening to the speaker's ideas and experiences - Identifying the central idea of the talk - Selecting the most meaningful stories and examples - Developing a clear narrative structure - Connecting the ideas to the TEDx format - Considering the audience's perspective - Refining the story while preserving the speaker's own voice

The Idea Behind the Talk

Mokri’s lecture explored his personal and professional journey, from the small and large choices that eventually led him to become a director, to the experience of creating a work of art.

One of the central ideas he discussed was the role of the director as “the chooser.” He explored the relationship between the author, the audience, and the work itself, moving between ideas from classical cinema and postmodern cinema.

He also looked at the audience from two different perspectives: the audience member who remains relatively passive, and the one who actively comments, interprets, and questions everything they see.

The talk eventually led to a larger question:

Is the author still alive?

This question became an important part of the talk’s conceptual direction, connecting Mokri’s personal experiences as a filmmaker with broader questions about authorship, interpretation, and the relationship between a work and its audience.

Learning From Eight Perspectives

TEDxUT brought together eight speakers from very different professional and intellectual backgrounds. Although my direct curating responsibility was focused on Shahram Mokri, the overall experience gave me the opportunity to listen to and learn from many different perspectives.

The eight speakers were:

  • Zhaleh Amozgar — Professor and researcher of Persian literature
  • Mohammad Reza Alavi — Civil Engineer and real estate professional
  • Akbar Soltani — Physician
  • Zahra Monsef — Academic and researcher
  • Roozbeh Salehabadi — CEO of Barfap Iranian Company
  • Mahoor Alvand — Theatrical and film artist
  • Shahram Mokri — Filmmaker and director
  • Hossein Selahvarzi — Entrepreneur and former head of Iran Chamber of Commerce

Being surrounded by such different perspectives was one of the most valuable parts of the experience.

Lessons Beyond the Curating Process

Working with Akbar Soltani gave me a deeper appreciation for critical thinking and the importance of questioning ideas rather than accepting them at face value.

From Mohammad Reza Alavi, I learned more about the world of real estate and the way this field connects to the development of cities and the built environment.

My exposure to Roozbeh Salehabadi also made me think more deeply about the importance of soil and the fundamental role it plays in our environment and the way we build.

And Zhaleh Amozgar’s speech was particularly moving. Her way of connecting knowledge, culture, and personal experience created a powerful emotional moment during the event.

These experiences reminded me that one of the most valuable parts of an event like TEDx is not only the final talks, but the opportunity to encounter ideas that are far outside your own field.

Learning Through the Process

Working with Shahram Mokri was one of the most valuable parts of my TEDxUT experience.

Our meetings gave me the opportunity to learn directly from someone with a very different professional background and way of thinking. I learned how a filmmaker approaches storytelling, how small decisions can shape a larger narrative, and how a personal experience can become a meaningful idea for an audience.

At the same time, the process taught me how to listen critically. Rather than trying to impose a structure on the speaker, I learned to find the structure that was already hidden within their ideas and help make it clearer.

## From Curation to the Stage The curating process eventually moved from conversations and revisions to the final recording and event. Seeing the ideas we had discussed during our meetings become a finished talk was a rewarding part of the experience. It showed me how much work can exist behind a presentation that, from the audience's perspective, may only last a few minutes. For me, the experience demonstrated that **good storytelling is not only about speaking well. It is also about choosing what to say, what to leave out, how to structure an idea, and how to create a connection with the audience.**

What I Took From the Experience

Working with Shahram Mokri changed the way I think about the relationship between ideas and storytelling.

I learned that curating a talk is a process of discovery. The goal is not to create a story for the speaker, but to help the speaker discover the clearest and most engaging way to communicate what they already have to say.

At the same time, being part of an event with eight speakers from different fields exposed me to perspectives I would not normally encounter. From critical thinking and cinema to real estate, soil, literature, and culture, each speaker added a different dimension to the experience.

This experience strengthened my skills in storytelling, communication, critical listening, narrative development, and collaboration. More importantly, it taught me that meaningful communication begins with curiosity — listening carefully enough to understand an idea before trying to communicate it to someone else.
