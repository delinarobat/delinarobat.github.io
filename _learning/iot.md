## How Does a Small Device Become Part of the Internet of Things?

**Self-directed learning project**  
**August 2026**  
**Tools: Coursera · UC Irvine · Introduction to the Internet of Things and Embedded Systems**

Notes from an introductory course by Professor Ian Harris (UC Irvine), the first course in the specialization *An Introduction to Programming the Internet of Things*.

## Why I Started

Much of my work is about buildings and the data around them, and I realized I did not understand how that data is collected in the first place. Sensors, small computers, and networks sit behind almost every "smart" system, so I wanted to learn the basics of how they work before going deeper into any one technology.

## What Is the Internet of Things?

"IoT" means different things to different people, so the course starts by defining it, looking at the trends that made it possible, and asking how it changes the way products are designed.

**Key takeaway:** IoT is less a single technology than a combination of cheap sensors, small processors, and constant connectivity.

## Embedded Systems

Unlike a general-purpose computer, an embedded system is built to do one job inside a larger device, often under tight limits on power, size, and cost. The module covers how such systems interact with the physical world.

**Key takeaway:** design constraints (power, cost, size) shape an IoT device as much as its features do.

## Hardware and Software

IoT devices combine dedicated hardware, which handles the physical interface and heavy computation, with software running on microcontrollers. The module looks at the trade-offs between the two.

**Key takeaway:** deciding what to do in hardware and what to do in software is a design choice with real consequences for speed, flexibility, and cost.

## Networking and the Internet

For a device to be useful it usually has to communicate, with other devices or with cloud servers. This module introduces how the Internet is structured, how data moves across networks, and why Internet protocols matter for IoT design.

**Key takeaway:** connectivity is part of the design from the start, not something added at the end.

## What I Learned

* IoT is a system of layers (sensors, embedded processors, networks, and cloud services), and a problem in any layer affects the whole.
* Embedded devices are designed around constraints, so "just add more computing power" is often not an option.
* Understanding networking basics is necessary even if you never design the hardware.

## Limitations

This is an introductory course, so it gave me concepts and vocabulary rather than hands-on experience. I have not yet built an IoT device myself, and a certificate shows that I completed the course, not that I can design a working system.
